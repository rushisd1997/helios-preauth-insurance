HOW THE SYSTEM DECIDES

Decision design & 15-minute SLA

The AI helps organize evidence and prioritize work. It does not independently decide that a patient should be refused treatment.

0–2 minutes

Validate request

Check required fields, policy dates, package codes and duplicate submissions. Return a clear correction request for malformed data.

2–5 minutes

Gather evidence

Run policy rules and extract facts from notes and documents. Keep source references and flag low-quality scans or uncertain extraction.

5–10 minutes

Route by need

Keep validated low-risk rules in the existing safe automation path. Route conflicting, uncertain or high-impact cases to a qualified reviewer.

10–15 minutes

Escalate and respond

Escalate urgent unresolved cases to the reviewer lead. If AI times out, continue through the existing rules and human queue rather than waiting for the model.

Decision boundaries

  • Rules engine: policy status, effective dates, package limits and required fields.
  • LLM: summarize notes, extract structured evidence, identify uncertainty and cite source passages.
  • Risk model: prioritize review workload; a score is not evidence of ineligibility or fraud.
  • Qualified reviewer: decides ambiguous clinical cases and records the policy basis for any decline.
  • Hospital: receives missing items, status, reason and a route to challenge the outcome.

What we measure

  • Inappropriate approvals and inappropriate declines, measured separately.
  • Time to first response and final decision; percentage completed within 15 minutes.
  • Appeal overturns, reviewer overrides and cases where review changed the outcome.
  • Document rework, hospital complaints, member grievances and differences across hospitals or groups.

The time bands are proposed operating targets, not measured performance. They must be tested against actual arrival rates, reviewer capacity and clinical urgency.
